# Broadcast output and synthetic tracking: design

Extends `2026-10-19-agent-crew-design.md`, `2026-10-19-ask-the-match-design.md` and `2026-10-19-voice-narration-design.md` (feature 4 of the roadmap). Goal: the crew's work reaches a real program output. A second route of the same SPA renders a clean 16:9 feed for a studio monitor or an OBS browser source, with the overlays exactly as the producer timed them, and a deterministic tracking layer turns the event stream into per-frame positions of the 22 players and the ball, so the pitch monitor shows a match, not a scatter of markers.

## Intent

Real-world impact and UX are two of the five criteria. Today the dashboard proves the pipeline to a judge; a broadcaster needs the output: a frame to put on air, with cues that come and go on the producer's schedule. Tracking makes that frame legible from across a room, and it is the kind of data (positions at a fixed rate) real providers sell, synthesised here from events alone, with no model and no randomness.

## Synthetic tracking: `bts.tracking`

Pure Python, deterministic for a given seed, no model. A `MatchTracker(meta, events, fps=4)` takes a match's full event list and produces frames at 4 per match second: `Frame {t, ball: (x, y), players: {player_id: (x, y)}}`. Coordinates are in the **screen frame**: both axes 0 to 100 as in the event schema, the home side attacking from left (`x = 0`) to right (`x = 100`) for the whole match (the UI's `Pitch` draws `y` scaled to 65 units, as it does for events). Only players on the pitch appear in `players`: the starting elevens, then substitutes in place of the players they replaced; a sent-off player disappears at the card.

**Frames of reference.** Events are recorded in the attacking frame of the side in possession, so the tracker follows possession to place them on screen: on-ball events (`pass`, `carry`, `shot`, `miscontrol`, `ball_out`) are in their own team's frame; every other event with coordinates (`pressure`, `foul`, `card`, `interception`, `recovery`, `duel`, `clearance`, `goalkeeper`) is in the frame of the side that had the ball before it. Possession after an event: `period_start`, `pass`, `carry` give it to the event's team; `ball_out` and `miscontrol` to the opponent; a `shot` to the opponent when `goal` or `off_target` and otherwise stays with the shooter until the `goalkeeper`, `recovery` or `clearance` that resolves it; `interception`, `recovery`, `clearance`, `goalkeeper` and a won `duel` take it; `pressure`, `foul`, `card`, a lost `duel`, `substitution` and `period_end` leave it. Checked over seven seeds: with this rule the ball only jumps after a goal (to the kick-off spot), an off-target shot (to the keeper's restart), a clearance and at the second-half kick-off, which is the simulator's own behaviour. The away frame maps to screen as `(100 - x, 100 - y)`.

**Ball.** An event's time `t` is when its action ends (the simulator advances the clock before emitting a pass, carry or shot), so the ball is at `end` (or `start` when there is no `end`) exactly at `t`; a blocked shot ends where it started. Between anchors the ball moves linearly in time: a pass or shot leaves `start` at `t - distance / speed` using the event's own `ball_speed` or `shot_speed` (km/h), a carry at 3 m/s, each travel clipped to the gap since the previous anchor; when the next anchor is elsewhere (kick-off after a goal, a keeper's restart, a cleared ball) the ball rests, then moves at 8 m/s so it arrives at the next anchor's time, or spans the whole gap when it is shorter. Substitutions and period markers are not ball anchors.

**Players.** Each player has a formation home in the team's attacking frame, a 4-4-2 over the simulator's `starting_xi` order (GK `(6, 50)`; DF `(22, 16)`, `(20, 38)`, `(20, 62)`, `(22, 84)`; MF `(44, 14)`, `(42, 38)`, `(42, 62)`, `(44, 86)`; FW `(64, 40)`, `(64, 60)`); a substitute inherits the slot of the player who went off and enters from the halfway line at the near touchline (screen `(50, 0)`), a sent-off player's slot stays empty. Every frame, each player moves toward a target at no more than **9 m/s** (105 m by 68 m pitch, so 2.25 m per frame), which is the no-teleport guarantee. The target is, in order: the player's next event anchor when it is at most 8 s away (the event's `start`, at the moment the ball leaves for on-ball events and at `t` for the rest, or earlier, at the previous anchor's time, when the previous event's ball position is this `start`, which is how a receiver gets there before the pass arrives); the ball itself while the player is carrying it (`carry`, between departure and `t`); the last anchor for 2 s after it passed; otherwise the formation target: home shifted with possession, `x + 0.3 * (ball_x - 50) + 7` for the side in possession and `- 7` for the other, `y + 0.25 * (ball_y - 50)`, ball coordinates in the team's frame, clamped to the pitch, the keeper staying within 12 units of the goal line. Three or so anchors per match ask more than 9 m/s; the player arrives late and the ball still keeps its time.

**Interface.** `MatchTracker.frames(from_t, to_t, limit=600) -> list[Frame]`: frames on the `1/fps` grid with `from_t <= t <= to_t`, at most `limit`, never beyond the last event. Frames are integrated in order and cached compactly (one `array('f')` per frame), extended lazily under a lock so a late window costs one pass over the earlier frames once; `ball_at(t)` evaluates the ball path alone. `MatchTracker.for_match(meta)` replays the seed with a fresh `MatchSimulator` (the simulator is deterministic, ~25 ms), so a live session can serve frames ahead of its clock without touching the generator that feeds the stream; the session builds it on the first request and keeps it.

## API

`GET /api/matches/{id}/tracking?from_t=0&to_t=150` returns `list[Frame]`, pydantic-serialised as `[{"t": 12.25, "ball": [51.3, 48.9], "players": {"t1p1": [6.0, 50.0], …}}]`. `from_t >= 0`, `to_t > from_t` (422 otherwise); `to_t` defaults to `from_t + 150`; the response holds at most **600 frames** from `from_t` on (150 s at 4 fps), so a wider window is cut, not refused; a window past the end of the match returns what exists, possibly `[]`. Frame integration runs in a worker thread (`asyncio.to_thread`) so the stream's loop never waits on it. Flag `BTS_TRACKING_ENABLED` (bool, default true): off, the route answers 404 `tracking is off (BTS_TRACKING_ENABLED)` and `GET /api/features` reports `tracking: false`. The SSE stream is untouched.

## Web: tracking layer

- `web/src/lib/tracking.ts` is pure and vitest-covered: `Frame`, `interpolate(a, b, t)` (linear per player; a player present in one frame only takes that frame's position when entering, and is dropped when leaving), `frameAt(frames, t)` (binary search, clamped to the ends, `null` when empty), and `TrackingBuffer`: `add(frames)` merges by `t`, `coverage()`, `prune(before)` drops frames older than 30 s behind the clock, `nextWindow(clock, aheadS = 90)` returns the `[from, to]` to fetch when the buffer ends less than `aheadS` ahead of the clock (capped at 150 s, continuing from the buffer's end) or `null`.
- `useTracking(matchId, enabled, clock, speed)` owns a buffer, fetches `nextWindow` whenever the stream clock moves and a fetch is not already in flight, and exposes `frameAt(now)` through a ref: `now` is the stream clock extrapolated by `(performance.now() - lastClockAt) / 1000 * speed`, clamped to the buffer, so the dots keep moving between events.
- `TrackingLayer` renders inside the `Pitch` SVG (new `layer` prop): one `<g>` per squad player (dot in the team's first colour, chalk hairline, shirt number in display type) and the ball (chalk, dark stroke), positioned by a `requestAnimationFrame` loop that writes `transform` attributes directly, never through React state, and hides players absent from the frame. Event markers stay as they are. Budget: at least 30 fps on a laptop with the match at 30×, measured with the Performance API (frame times over 10 s, reported in the plan's final note).
- `Pitch` mirrors defensive events the way the tracker does (possession before the event, derived from the events it has), so a save and the ball sit at the same goal. This fixes a visible bug in the current `DEFENSIVE` set and is in scope because the two layers share one pitch.
- Controls gain a "Tracking" pill switch (same idiom as narration), present only when `features.tracking`, remembered in `localStorage` under `bts.tracking`, off by default on the dashboard.

## Web: broadcast route

- `#/broadcast?match=<id>&profile=analyst&lang=en&narration=0`: `main.tsx` mounts `Broadcast` instead of `App` when the hash starts with `#/broadcast`. No controls, no feeds, no footer.
- Layout: a 1280×720 stage scaled to the viewport with one CSS transform (`scale(min(vw / 1280, vh / 720))`, computed by a resize hook), centred on the page's night background, `overflow: hidden`, so 1280×720 and 1920×1080 show the same frame with no scroll. Top band: the shared `Scoreboard` plus, on the right, a quiet "Agent crew verified" marker that lights up (floodlight, live-dot idiom) while the lower third on air belongs to a verified insight. Below it the `Pitch` fills the stage with `Overlays` (same cues, same `activeCues` timing, same components) and the tracking layer on by default (`&tracking=0` turns it off).
- Data: `getMatch(id)` first; a 404 or a `finished` status shows the waiting state, a centred "Waiting for the match" line in display type with the match id and, when known, the final score; the stream hook, cues and verified set work as in `App`.
- Narration: with `narration=1` and `features.narration`, a single "Start narration" button sits over the stage until clicked (autoplay rules), then `useNarration` runs with the switch on and the button disappears. Off by default.
- The dashboard's `Controls` show a "Broadcast" link (same button style, `target="_blank"`) to this route for the running match, profile and language.
- Aesthetic per `web/DESIGN.md`: chalk hairlines, Barlow Condensed numerals, team colours only on crests, dots and the lower third's bar.

## Constraints

- Behind the flag; `main` stays deployable with tracking on or off. The stream never waits on tracking.
- Tests first: `tests/tracking/` (determinism per seed, speed cap, ball at event anchors at event times, frame grid and window bounds, substitutions and red cards), `tests/api/test_tracking.py` with the app fixture (flag off → 404 with the detail; 422 on bad windows; the 600 cap; two sessions of one seed answer identically), vitest for `tracking.ts`.
- One Conventional Commit per task, nothing pushed. Code, comments, commits and docs in English. No real club, player or competition names. No audio through the speakers while verifying.

## Out of scope

Physical ball flight (height, bounce), player orientation, goalkeeper dives, a WebSocket tracking push, server-side video rendering, OBS scene files, a tracking export for the data pack (feature 7 can serve the same frames as CSV).
